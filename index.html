View-Dependent Proxy

How can I give the user the impression of holding a genuine opal, one of the world's rarest and most valuable gemstones?

The most direct answer is physical: a synthetic opal. It provides material fidelity and haptic feedback because the material itself supplies both appearance and touch. In a digital environment, however, the opal has to be rendered.

A render is inexpensive to reproduce, easy to modify, and open to inspection. Its touch and optical behavior must be reconstructed computationally. If you have a bit of insanity, you use a render.

An opal in AR begins as an exercise in curiosity: understand how a material produces its appearance, then reverse-engineer those effects under a different set of constraints. Like painting an object, reconstruction forces closer observation, and the process can produce insights beyond the finished image.

In my previous article, I explored how I rendered the opal's complex, view-dependent appearance. As of summer 2026, however, that rendering process is too computationally demanding to run in real time on current-generation AR glasses.

A proxy provides the necessary workaround. In computer graphics, a proxy is a lightweight representation that stands in for a more expensive asset or process. It preserves the required visual properties while reducing the computational cost of rendering and interaction. In this case, the proxy replaces live optical simulation with a set of pre-rendered views selected according to the viewer's position.
